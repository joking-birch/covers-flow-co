We don't manage ad accounts or bid strategy — that's its own specialized job, and there are people who do it well. But a huge share of what makes a Google or Bing ad campaign work or quietly waste money has nothing to do with the ad itself. It's what happens after someone clicks it. If that landing page is slow, confusing, or broken on a phone, you're paying full price per click for a guest who leaves before they ever see your menu.

Here's what actually determines whether ad spend turns into business, before a single campaign goes live.

## Quality Score is mostly about the page, not the ad

Google's Quality Score, the metric that directly affects how much you pay per click and whether your ad shows at all, weighs landing page experience heavily: load speed, mobile usability, and how relevant the page actually is to what someone searched for. A great ad pointing at a slow, generic homepage scores worse, and costs more per click, than a decent ad pointing at a fast, specific page that matches the search. The technical foundation isn't separate from the ad campaign. It's half of it.

## The pre-flight checklist

**Page speed, on an actual phone, on actual mobile data.** Most ad clicks happen on a phone, often on a cellular connection, not a fast office WiFi. A page that loads fine on a laptop can still lose a third of its visitors before it finishes loading on a phone. This is worth testing on a real device, not just trusting a desktop preview.

**A landing page that matches the ad, not just your homepage.** If someone clicks an ad for your weekend brunch, sending them to your general homepage instead of a page about brunch is a mismatch Google penalizes and guests bounce from. The page needs to answer the exact question the ad promised.

**A clear, fast path to the action you actually want.** Reservation, phone call, menu view, whatever the campaign is for, that action needs to be obvious and reachable in one or two taps, not buried in a navigation menu. Every extra step between the click and the action is spend you already paid for, walking away.

**Mobile-first design, not a desktop site that technically resizes.** There's a real difference between a site that was designed for a phone screen first and a desktop site that just shrinks to fit. Google can usually tell, and so can the guest holding the phone.

**Accurate, consistent information on the page itself.** If the ad is driving people to a location page, the hours, address, and phone number on that page need to match what's on your Google Business Profile exactly. A mismatch doesn't just confuse a guest, it undermines the trust signals search engines use to decide how much to show your site in the first place, ad spend or not.

**Clean technical basics: HTTPS, no broken links, no intrusive pop-ups.** These sound obvious until you actually click through your own ad like a stranger would and find the thing that's been broken for three months because nobody goes to that page except new visitors.

## The connection to everything else

None of this is specific to paid ads. It's the same Core Web Vitals and mobile performance work that makes organic rankings hold up, the same clean, fast, honest site that makes a hungry phone stick around instead of bouncing to the next result. Ads just make the cost of skipping it immediate and visible, every wasted click priced in dollars instead of buried in a ranking you can't quite see.

## The actual advice

Before turning on a campaign, or if one's already running and the numbers don't add up, walk your own landing page exactly the way a stranger would: on a phone, on a slow connection, clicking only what the ad promised. Whatever makes you wince is almost certainly the thing costing you the most per click.
