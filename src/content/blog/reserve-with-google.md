Every extra tap between someone finding you and someone booking a table is a chance for them to close the tab and pick whatever shows up next instead. Most of that friction happens in one specific place: the gap between the Google search result (or the Maps pin) and your own website's reservation page. Reserve with Google closes that gap by letting someone book directly from the listing, no detour required.

## What it actually is

Reserve with Google is a booking button that appears directly on your Google Business Profile and in Google Maps — right next to your hours, your reviews, your photos. Someone searching your name, or searching "Italian restaurant near me" and seeing you in the results, can see your actual available times and book a table without ever leaving Google, let alone finding and loading your website first.

It's not a separate reservation system you have to build and maintain. It connects to a reservation platform you're likely already using — OpenTable and Resy are the most common partners, along with a handful of other booking providers — and simply surfaces your real-time availability from that system directly inside Google.

## Why the missing tap matters more than it sounds like it should

Think about the actual path without it: someone searches, sees your listing, taps through to your website, finds the reservations page (which may or may not be obvious from your homepage), gets redirected to your booking platform's own site, and finally books. That's four or five decisions, each one a place to lose someone who was ready to book on the first tap.

With Reserve with Google, that whole path collapses into one. The person who was five minutes from a decision stays five minutes from a decision, instead of getting handed a scavenger hunt in the middle of it.

This matters most for the moment restaurants care about most: someone searching your exact name, already decided, just confirming you're open and grabbing a table. That's the highest-intent search there is, and it's exactly the moment a slow, multi-step booking path costs you a guest you'd already won.

## Getting it set up

If you already use a reservation platform that partners with Google (OpenTable and Resy both do), the connection is usually a setting to enable on the platform's own dashboard, which then links to your verified Google Business Profile. If you're not sure whether your current platform supports it, that's worth checking before anything else — switching platforms just to get this feature is rarely worth it if your current one already connects.

If you don't use a reservation platform at all yet, that's a bigger conversation than this one post, but it's worth having: the restaurants getting the most out of near-me search right now are the ones where the path from "found on Google" to "table booked" has as few steps as the actual walk from the sidewalk to the host stand.

## It's one piece of the booking flow, not the whole thing

Reserve with Google fixes the single biggest drop-off point, but it's not a substitute for the rest of the path being clean — your website's own reservation link should still be obvious, fast, and mobile-friendly for the people who do land on your site first. Think of it as removing the worst tap in the chain, not the only one worth checking.
