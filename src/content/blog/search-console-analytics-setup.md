Most restaurant websites go live and never get connected to the two free tools that would actually tell you whether any of it is working. Not because anyone decided against it — it's just the step that gets skipped when the site launches and everyone moves on to running the restaurant. Here's what you're missing, and what to actually do about it.

## Two different tools, two different jobs

**Google Search Console** tells you how your site performs *in* Google search — what people typed to find you, which pages show up, where you rank for those searches, and whether Google is running into any technical trouble crawling or indexing your pages.

**Google Analytics** tells you what happens *after* someone lands on your site — how many people showed up, which pages they looked at, how long they stayed, and whether they did anything that matters, like opening your menu or tapping through to a reservation.

Used together, they answer the question that actually matters: not just "are people finding us," but "are they finding us, and then doing something about it."

## Setting up Search Console

Verification takes a few minutes — Google offers several methods, and if your site is already set up with a domain host, a DNS record is usually the most reliable option (it verifies the whole domain at once, not just one page). Once it's verified, submit your sitemap so Google has a clear map of every page worth crawling, rather than relying on it to stumble onto all of them.

After that, it runs itself. The part that matters is checking it.

## Setting up Analytics

Google Analytics (GA4 is the current version) needs a small snippet of tracking code added to your site, and from there it starts collecting data automatically. The setup itself is quick; the part worth doing properly is defining what counts as a *conversion* — someone clicking "call now," opening your menu page, or submitting a reservation form. Without that, Analytics just tells you traffic went up or down, with no read on whether any of it mattered.

## What to actually check once a month

Neither tool is useful sitting open on a dashboard you never look at. Once a month is enough for most restaurants. Here's what's worth your actual attention:

- **In Search Console:** which searches are bringing people to your site (and whether any of them surprise you — sometimes a dish or a neighborhood term is doing more work than you'd guess), which pages are ranking and where, and whether there are any crawl errors Google is flagging.
- **In Analytics:** where your traffic is actually coming from (search, social, direct, somewhere else entirely), which pages people spend time on versus bounce off immediately, and whether your conversion actions are trending up or down.

Everything else — session duration, bounce rate by itself, raw pageviews — is mostly noise unless it's tied back to one of those two questions.

## Why this connects back to your Google Business Profile

Search Console and Analytics show you what happens on your *website*. Your Google Business Profile has its own insights panel showing calls, direction requests, and website clicks generated straight from the listing. Looking at all three together is what tells the real story: how many people saw you, how many of those took a next step, and which of your channels are actually seating tables versus just generating impressions nobody acts on.

## The point isn't the dashboard

Nobody opens a restaurant to become a part-time data analyst. The setup matters because it's the only honest way to know whether the rest of the work, the rankings, the reviews, the Google Business Profile, is actually turning into people walking through the door — instead of just trusting that it probably is.
