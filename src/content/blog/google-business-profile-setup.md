Your Google Business Profile is the single page about your restaurant that most people will see before they see anything else you've built. It's the box next to the map, the hours someone checks at 9:47pm before driving over, the photos that decide whether tonight's the night. Most restaurants claim it once, fill in the basics, and never touch it again. That's the gap.

Here's what actually matters, in the order it matters.

## Claim it, then verify it properly

If you haven't claimed your listing, do that first — Google will mail a postcard with a verification code to your address, or in some cases offer phone or email verification. It takes a few days either way. Until it's verified, you can't control anything on the listing, which means anyone else technically could.

If someone else already claimed it years ago (a former manager, a web developer who never handed over access, nobody remembers), you'll need to request ownership transfer through Google's process. It's slower than claiming a fresh listing, but it's worth doing right rather than creating a duplicate — duplicate listings split your reviews, confuse Google about which one is "real," and quietly cost you both ranking and guests.

## Get your categories right

Your primary category is one of the strongest ranking signals you control directly. "Restaurant" is almost never specific enough — if you're an Italian place, your primary category should say Italian restaurant. If you do most of your volume on pizza, pizza restaurant might outrank a generic choice even if it feels like it undersells the full menu. Add secondary categories for what else genuinely applies (bar, caterer, breakfast restaurant), but don't pad the list with categories that don't reflect real volume — Google notices, and so do guests who show up expecting something you don't actually do.

## Hours have to be right everywhere, not just here

A guest who shows up to a locked door because your listing said you were open doesn't come back to check twice. Set your regular hours, but don't stop there: special hours for holidays, temporary closures for private events, and seasonal changes all need to be updated as they happen, not after the fact. This is also where consistency across the rest of the internet matters — if your website, your Facebook page, and your Google listing all say something different, Google has to guess which one to trust, and so does everyone searching.

## Photos do more work than people think

Listings with recent, real photos get more clicks and more direction requests than listings relying on stock-feeling images or nothing at all. Interior shots, the actual plates, the bar — not a logo, not a menu screenshot. Upload new ones regularly; a listing that hasn't added a photo in two years reads as abandoned, to Google and to anyone scrolling past it.

## The Q&A section nobody checks

Anyone can ask a public question on your Google listing, and anyone can answer it — including someone who's never worked for you and has no idea what they're talking about. Check this section. Answer the real questions yourself (do you take reservations, is there parking, are you dog-friendly on the patio) before a stranger's guess becomes the official-looking answer sitting at the top.

## Posts are free, short-term marketing most places never use

Google Posts let you share events, specials, and announcements directly on your listing, and they show up right where someone's already looking to decide. They expire after about a week, which makes them easy to ignore — but a listing that posts regularly reads as active, current, and worth a second look next to a competitor whose listing hasn't changed since it was built.

## Treat it like a page on your site, because it functions like one

The restaurants that get the most out of their Google Business Profile aren't the ones with the biggest budget — they're the ones who treat it as live, maintained real estate instead of a form they filled out once. That's the actual work: not a one-time setup, but keeping it current the moment anything changes, the same discipline you'd put into any other page that's actually making you money.
